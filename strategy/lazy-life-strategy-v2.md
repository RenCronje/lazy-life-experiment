# The Lazy Life Experiment
## Strategic Concept, Business Model & Operating Function

*Version 2.0 — repositioned from "fund my sabbatical" to "the funded-creative-freedom company."*

---

## 1. The reframe in one paragraph

The old pitch asked a stranger to pay your living costs for six months in exchange for updates. The new pitch sells three things people already pay for: **a tool** that helps them turn ideas into shipped work, **a playbook** that makes funded creative time repeatable, and **a story** worth following. You are still the first subject of the experiment — that's the compelling part — but you are no longer the product. The product is the machine you're building and documenting while you test it on yourself.

The inversion: *"Fund the platform that makes creative freedom repeatable — and I'll be the first guinea pig, publishing everything."*

---

## 2. The concept

**Positioning statement**

> The Lazy Life Experiment is a public research and tooling company that tests one question: does removing financial survival pressure produce better creative work? We run the experiment on ourselves first, publish every result — including the failures — and sell the protocol and platform to anyone who wants to run it with their own people.

**What the business actually is**

Three engines, in order of when they earn:

| Engine | What it is | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Audience** | The experiment as serialised public content | Patrons (subscription) |
| **2. Platform** | Tool for posting, backing, and tracking ideas to shipped-or-killed | Members + platform fee |
| **3. Protocol** | The documented method, licensed or delivered to organisations | Sponsors + companies |

**What it is not:** an equity offer, an investment product, or a promise of returns. This matters legally and is covered in §8.

---

## 3. Why this version is defensible

The original concept had a genuine moat — the *story* — but no way to convert it. This version keeps the story and adds two assets that compound:

- **The audience compounds.** Each week of published log makes the next week's experiment cheaper to fund. Attention is the only marketing budget you have.
- **The protocol compounds.** Every cohort produces data. Data produces credibility. Credibility sells the next cohort. This is how residencies and fellowship programmes became institutions.
- **The platform compounds.** Once ideas and backers live in one place, the platform is the distribution channel for everything else.

The thing you are building is not a sabbatical. It is a **repeatable mechanism for funding creative work**, with yourself as proof of concept.

---

## 4. The experiment design — solving the credibility problem

The original pitch couldn't be wrong. This one can, which is exactly what makes it credible.

**Pre-registered protocol** (publish this before starting, so results can't be reframed afterwards):

**Hypothesis**
Removing financial survival pressure increases both the *volume* and the *shipping rate* of creative output.

**Primary metric**
Projects shipped per 90 days. Countable, objective, hard to fudge.

**Secondary metrics**
- Ideas logged per week
- External quality rating — three blind raters score each shipped project
- Self-reported cognitive bandwidth (fixed short instrument, same questions every week)
- Abandonment reasons — logged at the moment of killing an idea

**Comparison group**
30 creators recruited from the audience, logging the identical metrics without funding. This is what converts a diary into research. Without it, you cannot distinguish "I was funded" from "I was paying attention because I was being watched."

**Baseline**
Your own 90 days prior to funding, reconstructed from existing records. Flag the limitation honestly: retrospective, self-reported.

**Kill criteria — stated in advance**
If, after 90 funded days, shipped-projects-per-90-days shows no improvement over baseline *and* no separation from the comparison group, the hypothesis is published as **failed**, in full, with raw data. This commitment is the product. Anyone can run a vanity experiment; almost nobody publishes a null result.

**Publication cadence**
- Weekly: the log (what was attempted, shipped, killed)
- Monthly: the data drop (raw, downloadable)
- End of cohort: full report with methodology and limitations

**The trust mechanism: the public ledger**

Every dollar in, categorised; every dollar out, receipted; every project, tracked to shipped or killed.

```
IN    Patron subscriptions        $X
      Sponsor underwriting        $Y
OUT   Living stipend (capped)     $A
      Tools & services            $B
      Platform costs              $C
OUT   What it produced            → projects shipped / killed
```

The ledger is the single most differentiating feature of the whole company. "Fund my life" is unaccountable by nature. "Fund this experiment, here is every receipt and every dead idea" is a completely different proposition.

## 5. Business model

### 5.1 Consumer tiers

| Tier | Price | What they get | Why they buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reader** | Free | Weekly log, public data | Curiosity |
| **Patron** | $5/mo | Member feed, vote on which ideas get funded next | Participation |
| **Backer** | $15/mo | Above + quarterly data report + name in the ledger | Belonging + insight |
| **Underwriter** | $250 one-off | Fund one specific idea to completion, credited in the log | Ownership of an outcome |

Annual option at 10 months' price for cashflow. No equity, no revenue share, no returns — see §8.

### 5.2 Sponsor-funded cohorts

A named fellowship model. A brand or foundation underwrites stipends for a cohort of 5–10 creators for 90 days. They get naming rights, the published report, and association with a genuinely novel piece of research.

This is how artist residencies and fellowship programmes have always been funded. It is a well-understood purchase.

### 5.3 The protocol as a B2B product

The most commercially serious line, and the one the consumer experiment exists to prove.

**"The Funded Freedom Programme"** — an 8-week innovation sabbatical for 4–6 employees at a client company. Protected time, no operational duties, structured idea pipeline, all outcomes logged on the platform.

Why companies buy: this is adjacent to things they already fund — L&D budgets, innovation sprints, hack weeks, sabbatical benefits. It is sold against an existing budget line rather than as a novelty.

Deliverable: anonymised cohort findings report the company can point to internally.

*Note the naming discipline:* keep "The Lazy Life Experiment" as the consumer brand — it's memorable and it earns attention. Sell B2B under a plain descriptor. A procurement team signs off on "The Funded Freedom Programme"; it hesitates at "Lazy."

### 5.4 Platform fee

Once outside creators post ideas and receive backing, take a small fee on facilitated funding. Long-tail, do not build for it first.

## 6. The financial ladder

**All figures below are illustrative assumptions, not projections.** They exist to show the shape of the model and the arithmetic, so you can substitute real numbers. Every one is an input you can change.

### Stage 0 — Prove it for free (weeks 1–6)

You do not need $50,000 to *start*. You need it to *quit*. Run a 30-day self-experiment at zero cost: log daily, publish daily, ship one small thing per week. This tests whether you will actually do the work and whether anyone reads it. Cost: under $500.

**Gate to Stage 1:** 30 published days and ≥200 people on a mailing list.

### Stage 1 — Membership (months 2–9)

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Members | 150 |
| Average monthly | $9 |
| **MRR** | **$1,350** |
| Annualised | ~$16,200 |

Lean operating cost: ~$3,500/mo including a modest stipend.

**Honest reading:** membership alone does not replace a salary at this scale, and it is not meant to. It funds the *experiment*, not the lifestyle. Stage 1 exists to prove willingness-to-pay and to build the list for Stage 2. If you need the experiment to pay your full living costs immediately, this model says: shrink the living cost, or run Stage 0 alongside existing work.

**Gate to Stage 2:** 100+ paying members and one completed data cycle.

### Stage 2 — First sponsored cohort (months 9–18)

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Fellows | 5 |
| Stipend | $2,000/mo × 3 months = $6,000 each |
| Programme cost | $30,000 |
| Sponsor price | $40,000 |
| **Gross margin** | **$10,000** |

One sponsor per cohort. The published research report is what makes the sponsor price defensible.

### Stage 3 — B2B programmes (month 18+)

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Price per 8-week programme | $20,000 |
| Programmes per year | 4 |
| **Annual revenue** | **$80,000** |

At this stage the company is no longer dependent on the founder's personal story, which is the whole point. The story got it here; the protocol carries it forward.

### Combined at maturity (illustrative)

Membership $16k + cohorts $20k + B2B $80k ≈ **$116k/yr** on one operator plus contract help. Not a venture-scale outcome — and it shouldn't pretend to be. It is a viable, ownable, honest business that produces public good. Ambition is better spent on depth than on a valuation story.

## 7. Operating function

### Weekly rhythm

| Cadence | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Log the work: attempted / shipped / killed, how long, what got in the way | One ledger entry |
| Weekly | Publish the log + one short "what I learned" note | Audience post |
| Weekly | Ship one small artefact | The proof |
| Monthly | Data drop: raw metrics, downloadable | Credibility |
| Quarterly | Report + cohort intake | Revenue event |

### What the founder actually does

Roughly: **60% making, 40% documenting and distributing.** That ratio is the product. If documenting drops below 30%, the audience engine stalls and Stages 2–3 never open.

### Roles as it scales

1. **Founder / first subject** (now) — does the experiment and tells the story
2. **Programme operator** (Stage 2) — runs cohorts, manages sponsors, contract or part-time
3. **Research lead** (Stage 3) — owns methodology and the published reports
4. Everything else (design, dev, legal) contracted

Deliberately small. A company whose entire thesis is "small is sufficient" should not staff up to prove it.

### Platform scope — build only what the protocol needs

- **Log** — daily entry, public by default, private toggle
- **Signal** — audience votes and backs which ideas proceed
- **Track** — every idea to shipped or killed, with reason
- **Ledger** — money in, money out, what it produced

Nothing else until these four are used weekly. Every extra feature is a reason not to publish.

## 8. Risks and how the model absorbs them

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Securities regulation** | High | Sell nothing that resembles an investment. No equity, no revenue share, no returns. Subscription, gifts, and programme fees only. Any equity conversation goes through a lawyer and never appears on a public page. |
| **"This isn't a real experiment"** | High | Pre-registration, comparison group, published kill criteria, null results published. This is the entire defence. |
| **Audience never reaches critical mass** | High | Stage 0 tests this for under $500 before any capital is committed. If 30 days of daily publishing doesn't move the list, the model stops there — cheaply. |
| **Solo-founder dependency** | Medium | The protocol must be written down well enough that someone else can run a cohort. If only you can execute it, it isn't a business. |
| **"Lazy" branding blocks B2B sales** | Medium | Separate descriptor for enterprise. Consumer brand stays irreverent. |
| **Comparison group is hard to recruit** | Medium | Recruit from the audience at Stage 0; they are already engaged and already logging. |
| **Sponsor sees no ROI** | Medium | The anonymised research report is the ROI. Sell the report, not the association. |
| **Burnout — the idea eats the person** | **High** | Non-negotiable: stipend is capped and time-boxed, working hours are logged and published like everything else. An experiment about sustainable creative output that burns out its subject has disproven itself. |

That last one deserves emphasis. The whole thesis is that financial pressure degrades creative work. If running the experiment produces a worse life than the job it replaced, the experiment has failed — and you should say so.

---

## 9. The next 30 days

1. **Rewrite the offer.** Stop asking for $50,000. Start publishing.
2. **Run Stage 0.** 30 days, one small ship per week, free. Publish every day including the bad days.
3. **Publish the pre-registration.** Hypothesis, primary metric, comparison group, kill criteria — before you have any results.
4. **Open a mailing list.** Not a crowdfunding page. A list.
5. **Recruit the comparison group.** Ten creators is enough to start; thirty is enough to publish.
6. **Build the ledger in public from day one.** First entry: $0 in, $0 out, idea logged. It costs nothing and establishes the habit that differentiates the whole company.

If, after 30 days, nobody is reading and nothing shipped — you've learned that for under $500, which is the cheapest and most valuable result available.

## 10. How this maps onto the site you've built

The deck is already the right vehicle. It needs new content, not new structure.

| Slide | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Hero | The positioning statement + "watch the experiment" |
| About | The subject, honestly framed — including the working hours |
| Summary | **The pre-registered protocol** — hypothesis, metrics, kill criteria |
| Market | Who this serves: creators, sponsors, companies |
| Business | The three engines + the revenue ladder |
| Financials | The staged ladder in §6, with assumptions labelled as assumptions |
| Join | **Membership tiers**, not "register interest" |
| New page | **The Ledger** — the transparency surface; the strongest asset on the site |
| `models.html` | The platform: Log / Signal / Track / Ledger |

The current site says *"here is an idea, please fund it."* The new content should say *"here is an experiment in progress, here is what it costs, here is what it produced, join if you want to watch or take part."* Same design, entirely different proposition.
